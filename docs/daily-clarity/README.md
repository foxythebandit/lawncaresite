# Calendar and service schedules

A confirmed service schedule creates dated visits before the previous visit is done. The worker keeps a 90-day window current and catches up from the last recorded date. A week or month outside that window can be read without creating visits. The database worker runs daily at 09:00 UTC and calls reconciliation directly. An existing date collision pauses generation for the affected plan and leaves its cursor unchanged while other plans continue. Unexpected database errors roll back the worker transaction, with its cursor available for retry.

Each booking UUID remains the saved visit's identity. `occurrence_on` is its current cadence slot. `original_occurrence_on` records its original date, falling back to `occurrence_on` for older rows. Several visits can share an original date after a series moves. Virtual visits use the agreement, revision, and cadence date together. A stale revision returns a conflict before an action can resolve the slot.

A single move changes the actual date. Moving the whole plan remaps eligible cadence slots, carrying individually moved and skipped visits with their saved actual dates and states. A window-only edit keeps every date, rule segment, and generation cursor. It applies the window to the selected visit and untouched future visits while preserving individual changes, completed work and paid visits. A move that would put two visits for the same plan on one date stops before saving, including collisions with projected visits outside the 90-day window. If an existing move conflicts with a projection, the calendar shows the saved visit and flags the plan for review. A staff template price change uses the existing line-item command to update new and untouched future visits at exact cents. The migration itself keeps all existing line items. Individually edited visits, completed work, recorded payments and accepted quote snapshots stay attached to their booking IDs.

The first rollout starts generation on or after the Austin cutover date. Existing confirmed past visits stay overdue until staff records what happened. Existing monthly schedules keep their month-end clamp. A plan with no dated, identity-matched source or priced template remains available for staff review. Preferred-date requests wait for confirmation before generating future work. If the first confirmed visit is on or after the proposed second date, the agreement needs schedule review. Cutover also flags a saved move that would otherwise duplicate a regular visit. Staff can keep working with the saved visits and restart with an explicit date after them.

Staff calendar range and attention reads use private server RPCs. Today and Next 7 Days read their date ranges directly. The customer portal uses its existing ownership policy to show the nearest upcoming visit and the latest unfinished past visit. It reads completed and pending visits separately.

Use `npm run check` in the isolated local worktree. The schedule tests cover annual and temporary periods, leap-month dates, cutover preservation, missed worker runs, series shifts, stale revisions, and access grants. Native tests check the registered worker command, two concurrent reconciliation calls, an isolated schedule collision and portal ownership with real local logins. The planner's slice 2 replaces the Jobs list and uses the range and attention APIs.
