# Calendar and service schedules

## Schedule storage and generation

A confirmed service schedule creates dated visits before the previous visit is done. The worker keeps a 90-day window current and catches up from the last recorded date. The database job `crm-service-schedules` runs daily at 09:00 UTC and calls `crm_reconcile_schedules` directly. A week or month outside the saved window can be read without creating visits. An existing date collision pauses generation for the affected plan and leaves its cursor unchanged while other plans continue. Unexpected database errors roll back the worker transaction, with its cursor available for retry.

Schedule revisions store the cadence, effective dates, and arrival window. The current series price is stored with the agreement, and each saved visit has its own items and total. Each booking UUID remains the saved visit's identity. `occurrence_on` is its current cadence slot. `original_occurrence_on` records its original date, falling back to `occurrence_on` for older rows. Several visits can share an original date after a series moves. Virtual visits use the agreement, revision, and cadence date together. A stale revision returns a conflict before an action can resolve the slot.

A single move changes the actual date. A reviewed move of a simple series remaps eligible cadence slots, carrying individual date changes and skipped visits with their saved dates and states. **Edit repeat** handles dates for more complex rules. A window-only move keeps every cadence date and rule segment. It preserves individual arrival changes outside the selected visit. A move that would put two visits for the same service on one date stops before saving, including conflicts with projected visits outside the 90-day window. An existing conflict leaves the saved visit visible and flags the schedule for review.

Generation for migrated schedules starts on or after the Austin cutover date. Existing confirmed past visits stay overdue until staff records what happened. Existing monthly schedules keep their month-end clamp. A plan with no dated, identity-matched source or priced template remains available for staff review. Preferred-date requests wait for confirmation before generating future work. The customer’s schedule links to the pending visit so staff can confirm it and continue editing. An older schedule with a first date that conflicts with its saved next date can require staff review. Cutover also flags a saved move that would otherwise duplicate a regular visit. Staff can keep working with the saved visits and restart with an explicit date after them.

Calendar reads use Austin dates from `lib/calendar-view.ts`. `getCalendarEntries` and `getCalendarAttentionPage` in `lib/calendar-data.ts` check staff access before calling private database commands. Today and Next 7 days read their date ranges directly. The customer portal applies its ownership policy and reads the nearest upcoming visit, latest unfinished past visit, completed visits, and pending requests separately.

Use `npm run check` in the isolated local worktree. The schedule tests cover annual and temporary periods, leap-month dates, cutover preservation, missed worker runs, series shifts, stale revisions, and access grants. Native tests check the registered worker command, two concurrent reconciliation calls, an isolated schedule collision and portal ownership with real local logins.

## Calendar and visit workflow

The Calendar opens on Week at `/admin/jobs`. The date and Day, Week, or Month view are shareable. Staff sign-in restores the selected calendar view, date, and search, including the originating calendar from a visit link. Week shows all seven dates side by side on desktop and phone. Phone cards show compact labels and keep the full customer name, service, arrival, and status in their accessible names. Tap a card to open its full visit. Back to calendar restores the week and focuses that card. Visits at one property share a heading and open separate records. Exact arrival times are ordered chronologically within the timed group. Search lists visits by actual or preferred date, with undated requests after dated results. The Calendar uses the actual date for the grid, while preferred dates stay under Needs scheduling until staff confirms a visit. Overdue and Needs schedule review link to their own paged results, including detected conflicts before the daily worker records a reason. Today and Next 7 days show the same attention totals.

A visit shows its date, arrival window, price, and status before any editor opens. Mark done records completion. Payment is a separate action. **Edit date & time** and **Edit price** open focused forms with separate saves. The date form offers a choice to email the customer when email is available. The price form changes only the open visit. **Edit repeat** opens the repeating schedule for future dates and prices. The back link restores the calendar context. A calendar map loads its pins only after staff opens it.

A projected visit opens by schedule revision and date and uses the same editors as a saved visit. Viewing the page leaves the database unchanged. A deliberate edit through `editProjectedVisit` saves the visit and applies the change in one database transaction. A stale revision or a saved individual change prompts staff to reopen the Calendar. The operation ledger makes a retry return the same visit.

An explicit payment-link or email-reminder action prepares the saved visit before calling the existing provider. The provider request runs after commit. A retry uses the same operation key while the provider still retains it. Payment links save only against the prepared visit version, so an intervening price change prevents the old link from appearing on the visit. Missing provider configuration stops before any visit is saved. A failed provider request can leave the visit saved for staff to review.

## Services and schedule changes

Open **Add visit** on a customer to book for that customer. The form defaults to a single visit and fills in the first saved property when available. Select a service label, enter the agreed price, and choose **Repeats** to set up a schedule. Staff can create, rename and retire labels. Active visits and schedule summaries show the selected label. Past visits keep their saved item descriptions and amounts. Two services at the same property can have separate visits on the same day, each with its own price, status and payment records. **Set repeat** on a saved one-off can use that exact record as the first visit while setting a separate ongoing price. An extra visit is a separate booking with its own agreed price.

Repeating services can be weekly, every two weeks, or monthly. An end date is optional. Annual periods can cross December and January. A bounded one-time period takes priority for its dates and the regular cadence returns after it. **No visits** skips that period. A strict monthly day or fifth weekday skips months without that date. Last day or last weekday gives a date in every month. Arrival can be a window or an exact Austin time. Existing arrival labels are retained.

After staff makes a change to future dates or prices, the service editor previews its effects before saving. A date range, pause, resume or end also requires a preview. `reviewService` in `app/admin/jobs/schedule-actions.ts` submits the preview and commit. The database recomputes the preview while locking the schedule and affected visits. A successful retry uses the original operation receipt. Schedule revisions made in the editor record the staff user and operation. Future prices can use a custom amount or priced items. Notes and contact corrections retain the visit and follow the schedule’s dates and price. Individually edited date, arrival and price fields have separate protections. If a changed rule removes a slot with an edited visit, the preview identifies the visit and lets staff retain it individually. Older ambiguous exceptions keep all protections. Resume can restore eligible visits canceled by the schedule pause. Individual skips and cancellations remain in history. Completed work, paid records and accepted quote snapshots are preserved.

## Sent quotes and preferred-date requests

Each new email quotes one service with its agreed amount, cadence, items, add-ons and timing note. A custom quote can repeat at its entered price. `previewLeadQuote` and `emailLeadQuote` in `app/admin/leads/quote-actions.ts` use the same reviewed terms. The sent email and customer link share the frozen record in `sent_offers`. Staff see the recipient, time and terms in **Sent quotes**. **Text price** opens the staff phone's Messages app with the sent link. A draft edit leaves that link unchanged. A revision replaces an open offer after the email provider accepts the send. The original link still shows its original terms and leads to the revised quote. An accepted visit keeps its original terms if a revision is in flight.

An unconfirmed send can retry its stored email with the same provider key within 23 hours. The email is marked sent after provider acceptance. For older attempts, staff review the exact recipient, subject and time in the provider history. Staff can record a verified receipt or close an unconfirmed attempt with an evidence note before reviewing a fresh send. A closed link keeps its original terms and points to a newer offer when available. [Resend idempotency keys](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys)

A customer requests a preferred date. The existing intake transaction saves a pending visit and the staff alert intent once per offer. Staff can record an agreement from a sent quote or a phone call and review the same saved request. **Confirm first visit** shows the customer note alongside the customer, property, items and date. Staff can set the agreed items and price from the review if the request has no saved amount. `crm_confirm_first_visit` confirms the existing visit and starts the agreed series in one transaction. Phone matches prompt an eligibility decision before staff promise a new introductory price. Confirmation preserves the agreed amount and asks staff to review an existing property if its saved address differs from the quoted address. For a mixed inquiry, **Log call** asks which service was agreed. **Text draft price** and **Book visit** use the same reviewed first-visit and ongoing amounts.

Older links continue the request or show an existing visit. Some older emails lack verifiable sent terms. Staff review those requests against the original agreement. Prior multi-row offers retain every quoted row and stay in review until staff clarifies the work. Staff can arrange each agreed service separately.

## Introductory mowing price

First one-off and recurring mows can use the same introductory offer. A confirmed request keeps the exact accepted items and source rounding. `crm_intro_grants` tracks the unused offer separately from the original quote. It follows the first actual mow through a move or skip. A pause retains the offer. The resume preview, Calendar, and projected visit detail show the same first-eligible price, including dates outside the current generation window. Visit detail and the customer schedule summary also show the same next eligible saved or projected date. Starting a schedule from a one-off keeps the visit and its price. When an earlier series ended before any mow, staff can explicitly carry its unused offer into a new Lawn care series at that property. The review shows the saved first-visit price and the new agreed ongoing price.

Mark done waits for any earlier scheduled mow that could use the offer. Staff record or skip that visit first. A backdated completion that would affect an already-used offer shows both amounts before saving the actual date. An earlier extra visit explicitly added to the service keeps its custom price until staff review a compatible introductory transfer. When the agreed extra has different items, Mark done shows the original offer and the actual work. Staff can confirm that the customer agreed to replace the unused offer, or record separate work and keep the offer available. Both choices save the actual visit at its agreed amount. Replacement restores an unprotected open visit to its saved standard price and retains the canceled original’s history. Payments and shared payment links keep their saved amounts. Review them on the visit before moving an unused offer. Historical completed records remain as recorded.

The test also covers Previous, Next, Today, notes, edit conflicts, separate completion and payment, schedule review, search, and projected edits. It cleans up its fixtures. Set `QG_SERVICE_EDITOR_ONLY=1` for the seasonal service editor, one-off cleanup and series-move flow. It checks conversion through sign-in, independent amounts and completion, and the save button’s clearance above the phone navigation.
